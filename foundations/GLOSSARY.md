# Glossary

This glossary fixes provisional field vocabulary. Terms may be refined by later versioned amendments; they are not universal laws.

## Revisics

The proposed science of how systems change the space of future changes that remain possible.

## Revisicist

A practitioner of Revisics.

## Revisic

Adjective relating to Revisics, e.g. revisic structure, revisic assay, revisic boundary.

## State

A declared description \(x_t\) of a system at time \(t\). The state representation is assay-relative unless independently justified.

## Transformation

A declared change that maps one state or system configuration to another, or otherwise alters a specified system object.

## Transformation structure

The object \(\mathcal T_t(x)\) used by an assay to describe transformations available from state \(x\) at time \(t\). It may carry more structure than a set.

## Transformability

The property or structure described by \(\mathcal T_t(x)\). It is not presumed scalar.

## Future transformability

The transformation structure available after one or more system transformations have occurred.

## Revisability

A context-dependent form of future transformability concerning whether relevant aspects of a system can still be altered, revised, reopened, or redirected. Revisability is not constitutionally synonymous with corrigibility.

## Reachability

Whether a declared transformation can be realized from a declared state under a specified interface, resource budget, horizon, and admissibility condition.

## Corrective transformation

A transformation declared to resolve, reduce, or revise a specified error or failure relative to an explicit criterion.

## Corrective reach

The subset or structure of reachable transformations that meet a declared corrective criterion.

## Response map

An assay-specific mapping

\[
\mathsf M:U\rightarrow Y
\]

from interventions or inputs to declared response observables.

## Corrective-response geometry

A possible local or global mathematical description of corrective responses, such as reachable response sets, tangent maps, singular spectra, costs, or directions. The term does not imply that one canonical geometry has been established.

## Quotient

A map or equivalence relation that identifies distinctions in a richer object.

## Future-operation equivalence

Equivalence defined relative to a declared set of future operations. Two states may be equivalent for one operation set and inequivalent for another.

## Safe quotient

A quotient shown, under a declared criterion, to preserve the future distinctions or operations required by that criterion. Safety is always relative to the frozen criterion.

## Structural loss

Loss of a declared transformation direction or degree of freedom in the exact formal structure being measured.

## Practical weakness

A transformation remains structurally available but is too weak, costly, delayed, noisy, or resource-intensive to be usable under a declared operational regime.

## Revisic boundary

A boundary at which a declared property of future transformability changes regime. The term is generic; the mathematical meaning must be specified by each assay.

## Revisodynamics

A proposed subfield concerned with how revisic structures evolve through time. The name is provisional and does not imply an established mature theory.

## Revisic geometry

A proposed subfield concerned with geometric descriptions of transformation and response structures. Local differential geometry is one possible tool, not the constitutional definition.

## Revisic topology

A proposed subfield concerned with reachability, connectedness, separation, path loss, reopening, and related topological structure in future transformation spaces.

## Applied Revisics

Use of revisic questions and methods in a particular domain, with an explicit mapping from domain objects to the declared revisic formalism.
