# Research Lineage

## Status

This document records **antecedent research lineages** that helped motivate the Revisics field definition.

It is not a retrospective claim that those projects were already conducting Revisics as a named field, nor that their results jointly establish a Revisics theory.

\[
\boxed{\text{lineage}\neq\text{retroactive evidence}}
\]

## 1. Representation, quotienting, and future distinctions

Several prior projects studied whether present representations or compressions preserve distinctions required by later operations.

Relevant antecedents include:

- `matrix`
- `representation-revision`
- `signature-relative-equivalence`
- `future-sufficiency`
- `corrigible-compression`
- `correction-localized-predictive-representation`

In revisic language, these projects motivate questions about how present identification, compression, or representation changes the future transformation structure available to the system.

## 2. Reachability, interfaces, and corrective access

A second lineage studied whether relevant interventions, distinctions, or constructive states are actually reachable through a system's interface or policy-induced future.

Relevant antecedents include:

- `reach`
- `interface-theory`
- `revisable-computational-topology`
- `negative-space-search`

These motivate revisic questions about the topology and interface dependence of future transformability.

## 3. Correction, authority, and admissible change

Another lineage distinguished physical capability from warranted, authorized, or actually realized change.

Relevant antecedents include:

- `crank`
- `ssi`
- `PHASEONEbig`
- `correction-capable-adaptation`
- `the-correctable-lineage`

These motivate revisic questions in which the transformation structure is constrained not only by physical possibility but by admissibility, authority, or correction contracts.

## 4. Persistence, compounding, and self-altering access

A further lineage studied persistent adaptive state whose accumulated leverage changes the future evidence, interventions, or corrective routes available to the system.

Relevant antecedents include:

- `opencore`
- `cognitive-evolution-architecture`
- `cognitive-core`
- `adaptive-information-expansion-cycle`

These motivate the recursive revisic question most directly: when transformation today changes the conditions under which transformation tomorrow remains possible.

## 5. Measurement, provenance, and research-state discipline

Some repositories primarily developed methodological or infrastructural machinery required to study these questions without collapsing provenance or claim boundaries.

Relevant antecedents include:

- `white-rabbit`
- `cerebro`
- `corrigibility-distillation`

These are not automatically Revisics experiments. They contribute measurement, custody, reconstruction, or synthesis infrastructure that may support Revisics research.

## 6. Applied or neighboring systems

Projects such as

- `alphafold3-oc`
- `xm-oc`
- `cars`
- `magikarp`

may be interpretable as applied or candidate revisic systems when an explicit mapping to a revisic question is made.

Other projects, including `aimo-interp`, `ml-didacticism`, and `tevpp`, are better treated as neighboring research or instrumentation unless the recursive transformation-of-transformability object is explicitly instantiated.

## 7. What the lineage does establish

The lineage establishes only a historical fact about the research program's convergence: multiple prior projects independently encountered questions about persistence, representation, reachability, quotienting, correction, and future operations.

The field proposal observes that many of those questions can be organized around a shared research object:

\[
\boxed{\text{how transformations alter the transformations that remain possible afterward}.}
\]

Whether that organization supports common laws, invariants, geometries, or cross-domain theory remains an open scientific question.
