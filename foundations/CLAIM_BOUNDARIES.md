# Claim Boundaries

These are constitutional limits on interpretation in the Revisics repository.

## 1. Field definition is not evidence

\[
\boxed{\text{field definition}\neq\text{empirical confirmation}}
\]

Defining a research object does not establish a law, invariant, universal metric, or successful theory of that object.

## 2. Lineage is not retroactive evidence

\[
\boxed{\text{lineage}\neq\text{retroactive evidence}}
\]

Antecedent repositories retain their original provenance, contracts, null results, and claim ceilings. They are not silently converted into evidence for Revisics merely because they can be described in revisic language after the fact.

## 3. Common language is not common mechanism

Two phenomena may admit a shared formal description without sharing a causal mechanism.

\[
\boxed{\text{common representation}\neq\text{common cause}}
\]

Any mechanistic unification must be independently established.

## 4. Local is not global

Local response geometry, tangent maps, Jacobians, singular values, and finite-order jets do not automatically characterize global reachable transformation structure.

\[
\boxed{\text{local equivalence}\neq\text{finite-domain equivalence}\neq\text{global equivalence}}
\]

## 5. Structural existence is not practical usability

A transformation can remain structurally available while becoming prohibitively weak, costly, delayed, noisy, inaccessible, or fragile under finite resources.

\[
\boxed{\text{structurally possible}\neq\text{practically usable}}
\]

Studies must state which notion they measure.

## 6. Reachable is protocol-relative

A claim that a transformation is reachable is incomplete without the declared interface, resources, horizon, costs, admissibility rules, and system state under which reachability is evaluated.

## 7. Transformability is not value

More future transformation is not constitutionally better.

A catastrophe may increase some reachable possibilities while destroying viability. A useful compression may reduce redundant transformations while preserving every future operation that matters.

\[
\boxed{\text{transformability}\neq\text{benefit}\neq\text{viability}}
\]

## 8. Correction is a typed special case

A transformation is corrective only relative to a declared error, objective, contract, evidence condition, or other criterion. “Correction” must not be inferred from movement alone.

## 9. Failure localizes

A null or failed quotient at one layer does not collapse the entire research program.

\[
\boxed{\text{null at layer }k\neq\text{failure of every higher or neighboring object}}
\]

The permitted interpretation is the narrowest one supported by the failed control or measurement.

## 10. Control mismatch stops interpretation

Any preregistered experiment may impose stronger stop rules. Where it does, those rules dominate.

A failed identity, provenance, implementation, diagnostic, theorem-control, or assay-control gate does not become a scientific result about the target phenomenon unless the protocol explicitly says so.

## 11. Semantic categories are earned, not imported

Terms such as representation, authority, corrigibility, topology, control, intelligence, and viability carry domain-specific meaning. A toy positional stage or algebraic factor does not instantiate those semantics merely because it is given the same label.

## 12. No universal law by naming

The following remain open until established:

- whether Revisics admits universal invariants;
- whether a useful universal metric of revisability exists;
- whether different domains collapse onto common dimensionless coordinates;
- whether local corrective geometries generalize across mechanisms or systems;
- whether the field requires one mathematical formalism or several irreducible ones.

The repository may propose such hypotheses, but proposal and establishment remain separate statuses.
