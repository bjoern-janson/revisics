# Field Definition

## 1. Field statement

**Revisics** is the proposed science of how systems change the space of future changes that remain possible.

Its distinctive object is not merely a state transition

\[
x_t \rightarrow x_{t+1},
\]

but the induced change in future transformability

\[
\boxed{\mathcal T_t(x_t) \rightarrow \mathcal T_{t+1}(x_{t+1})}.
\]

Here \(\mathcal T_t(x)\) is intentionally generic: it may be a set, graph, topology, response family, controllability structure, admissible intervention class, representational distinction structure, or another declared object describing transformations available from \(x\) at time \(t\).

Revisics does not assume in advance that all such objects reduce to one common representation.

## 2. Distinctive question

The field asks:

> **How does what a system does change what can still be done?**

Equivalent research forms include:

- Which future transformations are created, removed, weakened, strengthened, redirected, delayed, or made more costly by a present transformation?
- Which distinctions must survive a transformation for future operations to remain possible?
- When does adaptive gain preserve, compress, or destroy future revisability?
- When are two current states equivalent for present behavior but inequivalent for future transformation?

## 3. What Revisics is not

Revisics is not defined as:

- a theory of AI alignment;
- a synonym for corrigibility;
- a universal theory of intelligence;
- a claim that every system should remain maximally changeable;
- a commitment to a universal scalar of revisability;
- a restatement of control theory, information theory, causal inference, dynamical systems, or constructor theory.

It may borrow mathematics from neighboring fields where justified. Its proposed research object is the recursive dependence between transformation and future transformability.

## 4. Core distinction

Ordinary analysis often fixes a space of available transformations and studies motion within it.

Revisics asks when the space itself is altered by the motion:

\[
(x_t,\mathcal T_t)\longrightarrow(x_{t+1},\mathcal T_{t+1}).
\]

The field is therefore especially concerned with systems in which learning, compression, representation, control, authority, memory, adaptation, or self-modification changes the structure of later possibilities.

## 5. Correction as a special case

Correction is important but not definitional.

If \(\mathcal C_t(x)\subseteq\mathcal T_t(x)\) denotes a declared class of corrective transformations, then one revisic question is whether a transformation preserves the future availability of transformations in \(\mathcal C\).

But Revisics also includes constructive expansion, safe compression, representational differentiation, reachability growth, and other changes to future transformability that are not naturally described as correction.

## 6. Domain neutrality

The field is intended to be domain-neutral in principle. Candidate domains include artificial adaptive systems, biological systems, institutions, scientific processes, computational architectures, and other systems whose transformations alter later transformation structure.

Domain application is never automatic. A revisic claim requires an explicit mapping from the domain to the declared revisic objects.

## 7. Constitutional inscription

\[
\boxed{\textbf{Revisics studies not merely what a system can do, but how what it does changes what can still be done.}}
\]
